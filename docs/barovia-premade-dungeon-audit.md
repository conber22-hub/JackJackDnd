# Barovia premade dungeon audit

This pass uses the 2016 adventure as the reference for classic maps. Strahd
rules Barovia and toys with the people trapped there. The Dark Powers gave
him the prison he calls a kingdom. The Amber Temple puts the vestiges and
their bargains in front of the party. Their gifts are played from the book
at the DM’s discretion. The Flood campaign keeps the Bloom as a separate
threat with its own scenes and creatures.

The maps use original, compressed grid geometry. Room names and DM notes
reference the published locations. Use [Appendix B](https://media.wizards.com/2016/downloads/DND/Curse%20of%20Strahd%20Introductory%20Adventure.pdf)
and [Chapter 13](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/cos/the-amber-temple) for
the full adventure, treasure, encounters, and dark gifts.

## Death House, room by room

1. **Portico and ground floor.** Run Rose and Thorn’s plea at the threshold.
   The clean dining room, kitchen and Den of Wolves establish a household
   that keeps waiting for its next guests.
2. **Library storey.** The family portrait and desk key send the party in
   different directions. The hidden study contains the lead to Strahd’s
   letter. Play it as the Count’s disdain for the cult, even while their
   shrine below still treats him as their saviour.
3. **Bedrooms.** Set the armour on the landing. The nursery and the
   nursemaid’s room make Walter part of the family’s story. Discovering
   the concealed route behind the mirror opens the attic.
4. **Attic.** The children and the dollhouse give the party a reason to
   believe the miniature door. When the book’s cue is met, the DM uses
   **Build → Door** on the hidden tile between the Children’s Room and the
   Spiral Shaft. Ordinary searches cannot open that tile early.
5. **Family crypts.** The crypt inscriptions tie the people upstairs to
   the graves below. The chanting draws the party past the cult’s quarters
   and into the shrine. The pit lies where the footprints stop. Play the
   children’s resting places if the party brings them down.
6. **Ritual vault.** The wet floor is difficult terrain and the altar has
   dry access. Use the door control to lift the portcullis. The party
   chooses whether to appease or defy the cult. The DM runs the aftermath
   and the house’s retaliation during the escape. Flood Edition uses the
   same choice and swaps the encounter for the Bloom Nest.

The map engine links adjacent floors by one paired stair tile. The VTT
uses its next-floor control for the first three stair flights, which lead
upstairs in the fiction. Run the one-way escape hatch as a DM move from
the lower dungeon to the Den of Wolves. The service lift, secret letters,
ghost dialogue, altar choice and house’s changing escape hazards are
handled at the table. The classic template starts with no enemies placed.

## Amber Temple at the table

- **Arrival.** Start on the mountain facade. The overlook gives the first
  view into the hall. A voice or spell can come from the faceless statue
  well before anyone knows Neferon is there.
- **The bargain.** Let Exethanter answer what his memory permits. Contrast
  what the party hears from him with what each vestige offers in private.
  The principal vault places Vampyr to the west, Tenebrous to the south,
  and Zhudun to the east. Their powers, conditions and costs are adjudicated
  from the book. The VTT keeps the offer cues on the DM side.
- **Travel.** The playable stair represents the descent from the overlook.
  Use a DM move for the secondary stairs, fissure routes that cross floors,
  and the Ravenloft teleport when called for. The temple opens through
  the template picker. Tsolenka Pass and an atlas route into the temple
  are future map work.

## Saved games

Use a fresh template to get these layouts. Existing maps on the shelf retain
their rooms, party positions and doors. An older four-floor Death House can
still be finished on its own maps. The current Story notes use room names
rather than telling the DM to use the old floor numbers.
